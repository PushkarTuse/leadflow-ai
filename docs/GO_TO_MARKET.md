# First fourteen days, questionnaire and review-only sales drafts

The objective is a first genuine customer through a narrow useful offer. All outreach below is a human-reviewed draft for an appropriate channel; no message has been sent and no mass sequence is proposed.

## Fourteen-day checklist

| Days | Owner action | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Walk through the property demo; choose a personal venture name/contact route; review employment and conflict terms | Approved working offer, truthful demo and launch prerequisites |
| 3–4 | Connect a private Sheet and Telegram with one synthetic enquiry; connect/test the real hosted contact form | One tested alert/approval/draft; one real form test from the owner |
| 5–6 | Review a small set of legitimate opt-in/platform opportunities; prioritize clear paid intent and useful scope | Actual discovered, qualified and permission-reviewed leads |
| 7–8 | Manually submit two or three specific proposals through allowed channels after reviewing drafts | Submission links, times, scope hypotheses; no assumed responses |
| 9–10 | Hold any invited discovery conversations; show the demo; ask the questionnaire below | Verified requirements and actual calls, or an honest zero |
| 11–12 | Offer one bounded pilot with written deliverables, assumptions, payment terms and account ownership | Owner-reviewed quote and acceptance checks; no unsupported deadline |
| 13–14 | Review actual replies and objections; improve the offer and demo; follow up only when permitted | Real funnel counts and one concrete next experiment |

Do not buy bidding credits, paid models, domains or telephony as a prerequisite to proving interest. If a platform needs paid access, decide separately whether that expense is justified. If no replies arrive, improve fit and specificity rather than increasing unsolicited volume.

## Requirements questionnaire

1. What service do you sell, to whom, and in which location/language?
2. What should a visitor do: enquire, request a callback, schedule or something else?
3. What happens to enquiries today? Which details or next steps get lost?
4. How many enquiries do you actually receive in a typical week? Unknown is a valid answer.
5. Which pages, content, branding, images and domain do you already own?
6. Which questions qualify an enquiry? Which information should never be collected?
7. Who should receive alerts and approve responses? What response hours are realistic?
8. Which CRM, email, calendar and other integrations are required? Who can authorize access?
9. What setup budget and preferred launch window are realistic, subject to scope?
10. What ongoing updates/monitoring do you need? Who owns maintenance tasks?
11. What would demonstrate acceptance: a test enquiry, alert, draft, booked consultation or another outcome?
12. May we contact you about this project using the channel you provide? Record specific purpose, channel, date and any withdrawal.

## Draft 1: response to an inbound local-business request

> Hello [name], thanks for asking about [specific need]. A practical first scope could be [pages] plus an enquiry form that captures [key fields], records the details and alerts [owner]. I can show a fictional property-business walkthrough so you can see how the approval step works. What currently happens after an enquiry arrives, and which tools do you want to keep? After that, we can agree deliverables and a fixed scope before discussing final price or timing.

Use only when responding to an enquiry or another permitted contact. Replace every bracketed field with verified details. Do not imply a customer relationship that does not exist.

## Draft 2: permitted freelance-platform project proposal

> Hello [public client name, if known], your request for [specific requirement] looks suited to a focused first milestone: [deliverable with an observable acceptance check]. I suggest confirming [integration/access], building one complete flow and reviewing it with you before expanding scope. Are [two relevant constraints] already decided? I can share a clearly labeled demo of an enquiry workflow. Final price and timeline would follow your answers and the agreed scope.

Submit manually through the platform after checking listing availability, proposal costs and applicable rules. Do not move communication off-platform against policy. The software-generated draft adds source context and explicit unknowns; remove internal review notes before any manual submission.

## Draft 3: permission-based referral request

> Hi [existing contact], I’m developing a small website-and-enquiry workflow service for [segment]. There’s a working fictional demo that shows how a website enquiry becomes an organized owner-reviewed follow-up. If someone in your network has already mentioned this problem, would you be comfortable asking whether they would like an introduction? Please check with them before sharing their contact details. No pressure if it is not relevant.

Use only in a suitable existing relationship; this is not a template for bulk cold messages.

## Quote boundaries

Write down deliverables, exclusions, number of revision rounds, content dependencies, account ownership, data handling, milestones, acceptance method, price/taxes, external costs, support hours and change-request process. An initial quote is the owner's business decision; the system does not negotiate or commit on their behalf.
